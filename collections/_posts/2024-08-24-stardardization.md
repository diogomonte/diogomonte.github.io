Many of us have joined different teams throughout our careers, and every team has its own characteristics, processes, 
code standards, testing strategies, and, of course, different people. These aspects may vary depending on the industry, 
company culture, company age, or, in many cases, the maturity of the team. Many teams have the freedom to define 
their own processes and ways of working, but often, team members are not aligned. When team members are not aligned on 
standards, processes, and testing practices, it often directly impacts the quality of the work and leads to an increase 
in technical debt.

Have you ever joined a team where these elements are not in place? Imagine your first day in a new position. 
You get a tour of the office, are introduced to your new colleagues, receive an initial introduction to the system 
you’ll be working with, and now it’s time to get hands-on. You check out the project from version control, 
but often the application has no documentation, and the README file is empty. You try to build, but the tests fail 
on your local machine due to missing configurations or external dependencies. If you’re lucky, one of your teammates 
will have all the configurations handy to share with you, but if you’re unlucky, you may have to talk to different 
colleagues, if they are available, to finally build the application locally. This process can be particularly 
frustrating in the world of microservices, where you might have to repeat this process over and over again as you 
work with each service your team is responsible for. This is not a good first day at work. I’ve experienced this 
scenario multiple times throughout my career.

For this reason, I strongly believe in standardized ways of working, where the team’s output can be 
predicted—not because everyone is working like a robot, but because there are standards. The team understands 
where the quality bar is set, and everyone is on the same page, while still having plenty of room for learning 
and creativity.

There is a difference between individuals working as a team and a team thriving and moving forward together. For this 
reason, I strongly believe in standardized ways of working. I refer to standardization as a set of rules, practices, 
processes, and coding conventions that all team members should follow. 

## The Benefits of Standardization

#### 1. Consistency
Standardization ensures all team members follow the same practices. In the long run, 
the code becomes easier to understand regardless of who wrote it.

#### 2. Faster Onboarding
New team members can get up to speed more quickly when there are established guidelines documenting practices and processes.

#### 3. Improve Code Quality
Standard practices can cover a range of topics such as design patterns, testing strategies, naming conventions, 
package structure, logging, and monitoring. All these aspects, combined with a common understanding among team members, 
result in improved code quality.

#### 4. Easier Code Reviews
When the outcomes of the work are predictable, code reviews become easier and more reliable. 
The team can focus on the application's business logic instead of being distracted by different coding styles and practices.

#### 5. Enhanced Collaboration
Standardization encourages documentation and knowledge sharing. Keeping documentation up to date 
promotes an environment of collective growth and improvement. Communication becomes more effective when 
discussing problems, and friction is reduced as expectations and quality standards are documented and agreed upon upfront. 

#### 6. Efficient Maintenance
Standardized code is easier to maintain. Removing distractions caused by different coding styles across 
the codebase helps identify and fix issues more efficiently.

#### 7. Reduced Technical Debt
Standardized practices help avoid building up technical debt. By sticking to consistent coding and thorough testing, 
the team is less likely to take shortcuts or create subpar solutions that could cause problems in the future.

## Conclusion
Standardization can offer many benefits to a team, especially for new members. 
The key factors are building habits and maintaining consistency across processes and code standards. 
While flexibility and adaptability are also important, a balanced approach that incorporates standardized 
practices can enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of a software development team. It’s crucial to give 
team members time to understand the advantages of standardized ways of working while also involving them in the process. 
Encouraging creativity, inviting ideas, and fostering a sense of ownership in the team are essential. However, 
implementing these ideas in a new team can be challenging and may face resistance.
